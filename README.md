# efm-genomic-practice
Practice genomic epidemiology workflow on public _Enterococcus faecium_ genomes: MLST, vanA detection, phylogeny (BV-BRC, KBase, Microreact).
“Do hospital-associated _E. faecium_ cluster separately from non-hospital isolates, and does vanA track with them?”
**Abstract**
Publicly available _Enterococcus faecium_ genome assemblies were selected from the BV-BRC database using filters for good genome quality and completeness. A total of 22 genomes were chosen to represent hospital/clinical, animal, food/environmental, and healthy-human sources, with no more than one genome from any single BioProject to limit sampling bias, and two reference strains were included. Assembly FASTA files were downloaded from NCBI Datasets using their GCF/GCA accessions and imported into KBase as an Assembly Set. Assembly quality was assessed with QUAST and CheckM, retaining genomes with completeness above [95]% and contamination below [5]%, and species identity was confirmed with GTDB-Tk, which led to the exclusion of two genomes classified as _Enterococcus lactis_, leaving 20 genomes for analysis. The remaining assemblies were annotated with RASTtk in KBase. Sequence types were obtained from BV-BRC and verified for 4 genomes by querying the PubMLST _E. faecium_ MLST scheme, and clonal complex 17 (CC17) membership was assigned from the sequence type. Vancomycin resistance determinants (vanA, vanB, and accessory van genes) were identified using the BV-BRC specialty gene annotations and cross-checked against D-alanine--D-lactate ligase annotations in the KBase genomes. Of the 20 genomes, 12 carried vancomycin resistance determinants. Two phylogenetic trees were built: a protein-family tree using the BV-BRC Bacterial Genome Tree service with default settings, and a species tree in KBase based on conserved genes. The two trees were compared for consistency of major lineages, and the BV-BRC tree was visualised in Microreact together with source group and vanA/vanB carriage. Because the genomes are a convenience sample from public databases and the trees are protein-based rather than core-genome SNP-based, the analysis was used to describe lineage patterns and not to infer transmission.
